## 2026-09-02 - Session S126: the side mapping was inverted, and a scene is the unit of reading

- P1 #empirical-refutes-plausible-criticals: Both Council models asserted with confidence that demuxing audio from an interleaved 7 GB MP4 must read the entire file (Gemini: "will read the full 7.5 GB"; Grok put 6-9 s on NVMe). Measured: 0.9 s. The honest caveat is that the file had just been written so page cache may be doing the work rather than ffmpeg skipping video packets -- but the predicted bottleneck did not materialise, and the design had been shaped around it. Probe the cost before architecting around a predicted cost.

- P1 #two-samples-are-not-a-rule: I measured a 73 s clip through a sync-only STT endpoint, concluded the documented 60 s ceiling "did not bite", and logged an explicit caveat that 73 s was a sample and not a proof of the ceiling. That caveat paid: the 1096 s file returned 400 "Sync input too long". The lesson is not "I was wrong" -- it is that WRITING THE CAVEAT DOWN at the moment of the favourable measurement is what made the later refutation cheap instead of a redesign. Same shape as the handoff warning that "higher number = right" was a coincidence of two samples.

- P1 #fixtures-find-what-reading-cannot: Writing a fixture suite for my own tool found four defects that reading it had not: raw KeyError instead of naming the missing prior step; "three disjoint windows" that were not disjoint (clamped to the recording length they collapsed onto one span, so the agreement check compared a window with itself and reported a reassuring 0.0 ms spread); os.path.relpath THROWS across drives, which was the real configuration; and a stated invariant that only held to a rounding step because times were stored at 3 dp against an offset at 4 dp. The 0.0 ms spread is the dangerous one -- a broken check that reports success is worse than no check.

- P2 #the-discriminator-is-not-the-obvious-feature: To verify which camera was which I extracted a frame at the moment he describes the two sides of the path. It showed a steep ivy-and-bramble bank, and he calls the RIGHT side the one that "kind of climbs up" with "a lot of brambles, a lot of Ivy" -- so the frame read as a right-side view inside a file declared LEFT, and I was one step from reporting the mapping inverted. Four seconds later he says "The left side too." Ivy and bramble are on BOTH banks and discriminate nothing. BAMBOO is named for one side only. Before using a feature to disambiguate, check whether the source says it appears on both sides -- and prefer the feature named exclusively.

- P2 #audit-your-own-citations: The site-truth document claims a timestamp on every statement. Auditing my own "t=~" marks against the word-level ledger found two attributed minutes away from the words they cited, both under claims a later session would act on without re-deriving. One drifted because the timestamp was real but belonged to the NEXT sentence. A document whose value is its citations must have its citations checked, not just written.

- P3 #specialist-lenses-catch-the-generalist: Four independent lenses over one evidence set found FOUR errors in a document I had already audited once. The pond was not dry (I had called that "the single most important instruction in the walk"); the walks were 1.2 s apart not 18 minutes (I compared start stamps without subtracting a duration); the wall hole was future-tense fiction I had written as an observed feature; and my area comparison pitted zone 2 against zone 1s CADASTRAL PARCEL instead of its BUILT MAP. None of these were findable by re-reading my own prose - each needed a different question asked of the same evidence. The generalist pass and the audit pass both missed all four.

- P3 #verify-the-agent-not-just-the-claim: Every lens claim that contradicted my document I re-verified myself before accepting: I opened w1_t0390 and saw the duckweed; I did the creation_time arithmetic; I queried world-index.json, decisions.json and zones.json for the grid size, the strip-list and the unplaced tiles. All held. But the discipline is the point, not the outcome - an agent report is an input, and the one time it is wrong is the time it gets written into a document as fact. Also worth noting: the taxonomy pass rejected MY frame set as too small (394x700, useless for leaf margins) and re-extracted at native 4K. A subagent improving on its inputs is a signal the inputs were wrong.

- P4 #search-by-content-not-by-guessed-name: He said the screenshots were in the screenshots folder. I searched, found nothing, and told him they had never been saved - having "searched properly". Two compounding errors: I matched folder names against *screenshot* in ENGLISH and his is Cyrillic (Snimki ekrana), and I looked under OneDrive/Pictures while this machine uses OneDrive/Photo. I also read the right observation wrongly: his capture showed Snipping Tool open, which I took as "unsaved" when it only meant the tool was still open. When a user states a file location as fact, the prior should be that they are right and my search is wrong. Search by MTIME AND CONTENT across the tree, never by a guessed directory name in one language.

- P4 #four-estimators-disagreeing-is-the-finding: Asked to settle the zone axis bearing, I got 65.2, 45.4, 84.9 and 80.7 degrees from four estimators on the same image. The temptation is to pick the one with the best-looking elongation and report a bearing. What the spread actually says is that the estimators measure DIFFERENT THINGS - the principal axis of a serpentine is pulled by where its strokes lie, two extreme pixels are not a polygon, and my vertex detector found 81 candidates for a 20-vertex shape with text glyphs winning. I reported the range, excluded the cadastre 22 degrees, and left the blocker OPEN. A 20-degree spread that moves the far end 69 m is not a number to average.

- P5 #i-over-claimed-corroboration: I told the owner the side mapping was "PROVEN, NOT ASSUMED" by three independent lines, and repeated "settled three ways" twice more across the document. Only ONE was authority (his declaration). The filename clocks say nothing about which HAND held the phone. And the bamboo -- the line I leaned on hardest and reported to him as decisive -- does not discriminate at all, because the OTHER camera is also full of bamboo at that instant. I never checked the other camera before calling it proof. The lesson is not "verify more"; it is that a corroboration claim must name what would FALSIFY it. "Bamboo appears in the left file" had no falsifier until I asked what the right file showed -- one command I could have run at the time.

- P5 #validate-the-instrument-before-the-result: A duckweed-colour statistic gave LEFT 15 of 16 samples -- a clean, quotable, wrong answer. It survived only until I ran the mask against known frames: bamboo grove 36.7%, ivy bank 36.2%, against the actual pond 58.5%. It measured BRIGHT YELLOW-GREEN, and foliage landed inside the deciding range. Had I reported the tally first and validated later, I would have handed the owner a mirrored zone with a number attached. Validate the instrument on known positives AND known negatives BEFORE looking at the result you care about -- otherwise the result decides whether you bother validating.

- P6 #the-authority-can-be-wrong-about-their-own-declaration: DEC-S125-5 was HIS declaration of which phone was which, encoded in the tool as "the only authority, never inferred". It was INVERTED. The lesson is not to distrust him -- it is that a declaration made once, about a detail like which hand held which phone, is WEAKER evidence than the same person looking at a frame and saying what it is. When a mapping is unrecoverable if wrong, show him an ARTIFACT EARLY instead of carrying the declaration forward. I had the pair that settled it within minutes of the footage landing and did not send it for hours.

- P6 #a-scene-is-the-unit-not-a-still: I extracted frames at scattered timestamps and matched each against one sentence. He had to explain two moments I had already "read", because in both he had aimed BOTH cameras at one subject for ~30 s -- invisible in a single still, obvious across a stretch. His words: "you are loosing context as you as separating this too much". The fix was structural, not more effort: segment on HIS topic changes, carry every word and every frame of the span together, and let taxonomy/typology annotate the span rather than replace reading it.

- P7 #the-span-produced-what-three-spot-checks-could-not: Three attempts to corroborate the side mapping from single frames all failed and I concluded frame content simply could not settle it. Then the FIRST area read did settle it: at w=40 the left half is a wall of bamboo, and he names bamboo as the left side at t=85.4. The difference was not effort or a better instrument - it was the UNIT. A 66-second span contains a moment where the cameras happen to be held normally; a single still chosen near a sentence does not. When spot checks keep failing, widen the window before concluding the question is unanswerable.

## 2026-08-29 - Session S124: a one-line save bug that erased every player's progress on load

- P1 #the-symptom-that-proves-state-is-fine-may-not-read-the-state: A day of play appeared to survive a bug that had in fact erased the player's progress, and BOTH signals saying otherwise were DERIVED rather than stored. 'No cars' looked like proof he was in 1601; Traffic only draws when era==='2026', so it was proof his era had been ERASED. 'DAY 24' looked like proof his save was intact; RealmClock reads the realm's SERVER epoch, so it shows DAY 24 on a character created one minute ago. Everything that DID survive - character, items, world flags - survived because only `progress` was overwritten, which made data loss look like a teleport. When an indicator seems to prove state is fine, first ask whether it reads that state at all. ALSO: #fix-the-cause-even-when-the-patch-was-approved - the approved micro PDR was a boot repair plus a write guard. Both treat the symptom, and the repair ALONE WOULD NOT HAVE RESCUED HIM: once progress is wiped there is no contradiction left to detect. The cause only surfaced because writing the test for the patch meant loading a real save and printing what came back.

- P1 #a-round-trip-is-not-a-load: 12 guards and 3,215 save assertions, and NOT ONE of them ever loaded a save twice. Every test wrote a fixture, loaded it once, and asserted. The bug lived exclusively in the second load - the shape this build WRITES, read back by the same build - which is the cycle every player performs every time they close the tab. A suite that only ever loads can prove the reader and can say nothing about the round trip. ALSO: #three-of-my-own-assertions-were-defective-this-session and every one was found by PLANTING A FAULT, never by reading: one asserted the absence of a string that legitimately appears in the comment explaining the fix; one was a prefix that survived renaming the function it guarded; one was not UNIQUE in its file, so the wrong value would have been masked by a second occurrence. The third was exposed only because the injection could not make a unique replacement.

- SESSION #he-found-it-not-the-gate: S123 closed with a full handoff, 12 guards, 3,215 save assertions and an MCV pass — and the very next thing that happened was the owner reporting a save-corruption bug that had been live the whole time. Nothing in the gate could have caught it, because every save test loaded once and the bug lived in the second load. The lesson is not that the gate is weak; it is that a gate proves what it was built to prove and NOTHING ELSE, and the only honest close is one that says which questions were never asked.
