## 2026-09-02 - Session S125: the map made 1601, and four carried "facts" that did not survive their first probe

- P2 #a-truncated-grep-is-a-lying-input: I recorded a finding as OPEN on the strength of `grep ... | head -10` whose first ten hits were case-insensitive matches on "shipping" and "mapping". ALSO: THE HAND-WRITTEN LIST GOT ME TWICE IN ONE PRIORITY. Five parcels typed from memory; there are six on disk. - P3 #verify-the-close-not-the-claim: A knowledge base is worth exactly what it costs to be wrong once. - P4 #the-carried-blocker-was-never-probed: Three separate handoffs recorded a blocker nobody had tested: "the domaine map needs parcel origins authored". ALSO: A PLAN LEFT UNUPDATED BECOMES A LIE WITH A TIMESTAMP. S121-P1 sat in .claude/plans/ for four sessions reading IN-PROGRESS and blocked on the owner while five of its six items had shipped. He was the one who said "im pretty sure we did the neon database already" - and he was right. Archive on completion, not at some later tidy-up.

- SESSION #prose-is-not-a-verification-array: I spent the session insisting on planted faults and empirical proof, and then recorded all five priorities with a long prose check_method and ZERO typed assertions. ## 2026-08-29 - Session S124: a one-line save bug that erased every player's progress on load

- P1 #the-symptom-that-proves-state-is-fine-may-not-read-the-state: A day of play appeared to survive a bug that had in fact erased the player's progress, and BOTH signals saying otherwise were DERIVED rather than stored. 'No cars' looked like proof he was in 1601; Traffic only draws when era==='2026', so it was proof his era had been ERASED. 'DAY 24' looked like proof his save was intact; RealmClock reads the realm's SERVER epoch, so it shows DAY 24 on a character created one minute ago. Everything that DID survive - character, items, world flags - survived because only `progress` was overwritten, which made data loss look like a teleport. When an indicator seems to prove state is fine, first ask whether it reads that state at all. ALSO: #fix-the-cause-even-when-the-patch-was-approved - the approved micro PDR was a boot repair plus a write guard. Both treat the symptom, and the repair ALONE WOULD NOT HAVE RESCUED HIM: once progress is wiped there is no contradiction left to detect. The cause only surfaced because writing the test for the patch meant loading a real save and printing what came back.

- P1 #a-round-trip-is-not-a-load: 12 guards and 3,215 save assertions, and NOT ONE of them ever loaded a save twice. Every test wrote a fixture, loaded it once, and asserted. The bug lived exclusively in the second load - the shape this build WRITES, read back by the same build - which is the cycle every player performs every time they close the tab. A suite that only ever loads can prove the reader and can say nothing about the round trip. ALSO: #three-of-my-own-assertions-were-defective-this-session and every one was found by PLANTING A FAULT, never by reading: one asserted the absence of a string that legitimately appears in the comment explaining the fix; one was a prefix that survived renaming the function it guarded; one was not UNIQUE in its file, so the wrong value would have been masked by a second occurrence. The third was exposed only because the injection could not make a unique replacement.

- SESSION #he-found-it-not-the-gate: S123 closed with a full handoff, 12 guards, 3,215 save assertions and an MCV pass — and the very next thing that happened was the owner reporting a save-corruption bug that had been live the whole time. Nothing in the gate could have caught it, because every save test loaded once and the bug lived in the second load. The lesson is not that the gate is weak; it is that a gate proves what it was built to prove and NOTHING ELSE, and the only honest close is one that says which questions were never asked.

## 2026-08-29 - Session S123: presence hardened, guards that were not guards, and the map he actually asked for

- P1 #the-untested-layer-is-where-the-bugs-are: All four presence bugs lived in the ONE part of the file nothing executed, and that is not a coincidence - it is the mechanism. S122 reasoned 'a Durable Object needs a real workerd to run' and tested the pure functions instead. The reasoning was true and the conclusion was wrong: a fake state, a fake socket and a controlled clock were ~120 lines and turned every one of those decisions into something observable. Ask of any file: which part of this does no test EXECUTE? That is where the next bug already is. ALSO: #fault-injection-not-green-runs - six faults planted, six caught, and fault 3 reproduced the original bug verbatim ('got 64' of 70). A green suite proves nothing about a suite that has never been shown to fail. The same pass caught a bug in MY OWN new assertion (a.of('join').length === 0 was false - A legitimately held the join B's first step broadcast), which is the harness earning its keep on its first run. #measure-the-platform-dont-assume-it - the harness needed three globals and I probed for all three before writing a line: Node REFUSES Response(101) with a RangeError, and WebSocketPair and WebSocketRequestResponsePair simply do not exist there. Guessing any of those would have cost a debugging round.

- P2 #ask-the-tool-dont-maintain-a-list: bundle-check's input list had drifted in TWO directions at once - it omitted a live parcel AND never covered hand-edited sources - and the carry-forward's suggested fix (discover parcels dynamically, like the other guards) would have closed only the first. The bundler already holds the exact answer; `metafile: true` is one line and turns 7 maintained entries into 41 authoritative ones that cannot drift. When a guard maintains a list of what something else consumes, ask that something else instead. ALSO: #make-the-claim-true-rather-than-retract-it - the evidence freshness assertion was a tautology INSIDE the chain and still load-bearing OUTSIDE it. Deleting it would have lost real coverage; leaving the claim would have kept lying. Moving the question to the one position where the answer is not already forced did both. #print-your-own-coverage - the guard now prints '41 src inputs' and '9 comic files'. A silent DROP in that number is precisely how it went blind to zone1_2026, and a number that falls is a question worth asking.

- P3a #a-guard-with-a-lying-input: Two guards this session had CORRECT ARITHMETIC over an input that could not see the thing they existed to catch: bundle-check's hand-maintained input list, and journal-strip's label scraper with a closed allowlist of the five page keys that already existed. Both were cited in handoffs as the protection. When auditing a guard, do not check its logic first - check what it is allowed to SEE, and simulate the exact edit it is supposed to stop.

- P3b #ship-the-thing-not-a-drawing-of-it: I presented four map designs and the owner rejected all four in one line: 'they all look like shit'. He was right, and the diagnosis matters more than the rejection - every option was a DRAWING OF the place rather than the place. His spec was one sentence: 'just literally capture the current zone map'. The build already produced a perfect 448x608 render of every parcel; the whole feature was a NEAREST downscale of a file that existed. I spent a research pass and an artifact on style before checking whether the literal answer was already sitting in docs/renders/. ALSO: #the-technique-is-in-the-references-he-named - he said go learn how MapleStory and WoW do it. Both BAKE a bitmap offline rather than downscaling a live scene. That is not a shortcut around the technique, it IS the technique, and I would have got there faster by taking the reference literally.

- P4 #make-the-guarantee-structural-not-a-rule: The owner asked that the SERVER tab never show locations. The old page satisfied that by HOLDING every peer's tile and choosing not to print it - which lasts exactly until someone edits that page without reading the comment. A guarantee made of a missing field survives; a guarantee made of remembering not to do something does not. ALSO: #a-test-that-fails-on-a-refactor-is-pointed-the-wrong-way - section 15's source-greps broke when the lifecycle moved files, with behaviour untouched. The same test would have passed if the reconnect had been DELETED. Replaced with a fake WebSocket that drives it; six reverted behaviours all caught.

- P5 #apply-todays-lesson-before-repeating-it: The knowledge base needed a freshness check. This morning I fixed CF-S122-EVIDENCE-FRESHNESS-TAUTOLOGY, where a check ran AFTER the build step that forced its answer. - P6b #validate-the-content-not-the-status: Gallica answered a burst with 429 and a text body. Written blind, that page lands on disk as a .jpg and every downstream check sees a file of the right name and size-ish. The fetcher therefore checks the JPEG MAGIC BYTES before writing, not the HTTP status - and the same principle fixed five MCV bindings, where file_exists does not register as a binding and file_contains 'PNG' both binds the file AND proves it is an image. ALSO: #pace-by-default-when-the-host-is-a-public-good - the BnF serves the digitised national library for free and a research pass made ~185 requests. - SESSION #the-owner-is-the-fastest-correction-loop: he caught the wrong commune in one line after an agent produced a 370K-token report about Burgundy, and rejected four map designs in one line with the right diagnosis - they were drawings of the place rather than the place.
