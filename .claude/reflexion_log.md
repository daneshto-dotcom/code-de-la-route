## 2026-08-15 — Session S109: the site survey — measuring the starting zone against the cadastre before touching anything (1 priority, analysis only, nothing built)

- S109-1 THE ANSWER TO "THE SHAPE IS WRONG" WAS NEVER IN THE FOOTAGE. Daniel sent a 6m41s walkthrough saying the shape and dimensions of everything were off, and told me to calculate the dimensions and look at Google Maps. The footage alone would have produced another eyeballed guess. The project already held the real cadastre, and public satellite imagery turned out to be fetchable with no key. Projecting both into a local metre frame produced the error as a number — one building 13.5 m out and on the WRONG SIDE — and found the single line he had described four separate times and that the map did not have anywhere. Machine-checkable, reproducible, and independent of my reading of any video.
- S109-2 FOUR REPETITIONS WERE A SIGNAL, NOT A TIC. He said "45 degree wall" four times across the walkthrough. I had heard that in earlier sessions as loose description. It is the longest edge on the site and the map is entirely axis-aligned, so the one thing he kept saying was the one thing that was wholly missing. When the owner repeats a phrase across sessions, treat the repetition itself as evidence and go looking for the thing it names.
- S109-3 THE OVERLAY WAS THE CHECK ON THE MATH, NOT DECORATION. Drawing the cadastral polygons onto the satellite image is what made the projection trustworthy: the polygons land on the roofs. Without that check every table downstream would have been unfalsifiable arithmetic. Any coordinate transform should be shown landing on an independent image before its numbers are used.
- S109-4 STALE PROSE, AGAIN. map.json's realWorldAnchor describes BLD-2 at "cols 21-26 rows 0-4" and BLD-1 at "rows 17-21"; the file actually draws them at cols 1-6 and rows 14-18. S108's dominant bug class — correct for where it used to be — survives in the documentation layer, where no guard reads it. Verify against what a file DRAWS, never against what its metadata says about itself.
- S109-5 ANALYSIS PRIORITIES STILL NEED A PLACE TO LAND. He asked me to log everything; the parent repo is public and publishes to Pages, so the site's exact geometry went into the PRIVATE submodule under docs/ and the public reflexion entry carries the method only. Deciding where a finding is allowed to live is part of recording it.
- S109-6 **PIXEL-ARITHMETIC ON A SCREENSHOT IS NOT A METHOD — IT IS THE SESSION'S BIGGEST COST.** Four round-trips went to one failure: a mark arrives on an image, and the only route back to a tile index is estimating the crop bounds from a remembered landmark, guessing the tile pitch and dividing. It is unreliable, it is SILENT when wrong, and asked twice it gave two different columns. It was not a near-miss to be more careful about; it was a method that cannot be trusted. Fixed by building `tools/grid.py`, which labels every tile col,row over a render — he marks a tile, the tile says what it is. The seven bamboo tiles that had defeated two attempts were read off in one pass. **When a class of question keeps costing round-trips, stop answering it better and build the thing that answers it.**
- S109-7 **A PASS THAT RUNS BEFORE THE THING IT MUST SIT ON TOP OF. Three times in one session.** Bamboo placed in section 6 and painted over by `woodland()`; moved down and painted over by `tree()`; a canopy-clear that ran before the canopy it was clearing. Six of seven marks landed and the seventh silently did not, which is the dangerous shape — a partial success reads as success. The file already carried this exact warning about STO-1's roof, thirty lines from where I re-made the mistake. **Reading a codebase's scar tissue is not the same as heeding it.**
- S109-8 **CHECKING HALF A SET IS HOW A BUG SURVIVES BEING FIXED.** Told the tree crowns overlapped, I respaced them and asserted it fixed. It was not: I had checked the edge crowns against EACH OTHER and never against the avenue trees, and an avenue crown was still sharing a tile. The fix is not more care, it is a total check — the generator now asserts every crown tile is claimed by exactly one tree, from either source.
- S109-9 **THE OFFSET WENT INSIDE THE HELPERS, NOT THROUGH THE CALL SITES.** Extending the map six rows north could have meant renumbering ~100 paint calls carrying eleven rounds of S108 owner corrections — a hundred chances to reintroduce "correct for where it used to be" in one edit. Applying Y0 inside `fill`/`row`/`scatter`/`woodland` left every literal untouched. **When a coordinate frame moves, move the frame, never the call sites.**
- S109-10 **THE GUARDS CAUGHT WHAT I WOULD HAVE REPORTED AS DONE.** reach-check refused two return warps living in OTHER map files that the extension had moved out from under; it refused landmarks buried by new features; and by refusing one it exposed that the log stores had been built two rows tall and walled the yard into a corridor. evidence-check refused a craft rationale that read as a TODO, and refused an orphaned strike-frame under its cel_void rule. realm-check refused four new records for missing geometry and one for claiming owner_stated without owner_verbatim. Every one was right. **The guards are not a formality between me and shipping; they are the reason a claim of "done" means anything.**
- S109-11 **A NAME THAT CONTRADICTS ITS OWN APPEARANCE WILL KEEP PRODUCING INSTRUCTIONS THAT MEAN THE OPPOSITE.** `earth_shade` reads as trampled ground, `earth_worn` reads as ground in shadow. He asked for one and meant the other, twice, and so did I. Recorded on all three legend entries as "ask for the LOOK, not the name, until they are renamed" — and the rename is now its own job rather than something done mid-flow.
- S109-12 **SUPERSEDED ASSERTIONS ARE AMENDED, NEVER DELETED.** Owner corrections invalidated nine MCV claims from S108 and earlier in S109. Each was re-pointed at the new form with a note saying what the claim protects and why the coordinate moved. Deleting a failing check is how verification quietly stops verifying.
- S109-13 **THE GLYPH CEILING WAS REACHED, AND PAID FOR HONESTLY.** Every letter, digit and punctuation mark is now assigned. Rather than smuggle in a tile, `e` was reclaimed — it had pointed at art declared for six sessions and placed in no map — and the orphan it left was retired under the same rule that catches any unused art. The ceiling is flagged for the next session rather than discovered by it.
- S109-14 **THE WORKING MODE IS THE CORRECTION ROUNDS, AND HE IS RIGHT THAT IT MUST SCALE.** Roughly twenty marked corrections this session, each one a screenshot. He named the real problem himself: over a 34-hectare property, "i need a system that doesnt have me send you screenshots 100 times per session". `grid.py` is the first piece; a clickable tile inspector and a browsable library of every tile designed go to Council NEXT SESSION after Part 3. **The deliverable is not only the game — it is the process that builds it.**

## 2026-08-14 — Session S108: BLD-1's interior and attic, the first play mechanic, and eleven rounds of owner corrections (5 priorities, all shipped)

- S108-1 GREEN GUARDS ARE NOT A GREEN GAME. Six guards, tsc clean and the bundle guard all passed while BLD-1's interior rendered as a black screen with eleven stray tiles. dist/art/atlas.png was 1h40m stale: the dev server copied art ONCE at startup, so the browser held an 88-frame sheet while the bundle indexed 104. S107's lesson was the bundle stale against the art; this is the same lesson pointing the other way, and neither a Python render nor any guard reading SOURCE files could have caught it. Fixed in both places, and verified the new guard FAILS on the real defect before fixing it.

- S108-2 I HAD THE EVIDENCE EXACTLY BACKWARDS AND THE COUNCIL CAUGHT IT. I found a round stovepipe socket cut into the chimney breast with a cast-iron stove beside it and wrote into the PDR that it PROVED Daniel's hedged 'probably a heater ... all coming down from the same chimney'. GEMINI-AUDITOR: a neat round socket for an iron pipe is 19th/20th-c and proves a LATER stove vented into an OLDER flue. I was one commit from writing a false record into the database and promoting an owner's hedge to confirmed fact. The lesson is not 'use the Council'; it is that finding a satisfying confirmation of what someone hoped is the exact moment to slow down.

- S108-3 MEASURE THE PROPOSED FIX BEFORE BUILDING IT, AND BE WILLING TO FIND NOTHING. All four candidate tightenings of the observed grade were measured first and all four died - the S107 proposal fires on 0 of 104 claims because only 3 of 126 records have children. Shipping it would have been a guard that cannot fail, dressed as rigour. I also caught myself resting a result on geom_frame, which is a coordinate frame and not a video frame. Four refutations and one narrow guard is a better session than four rules that assert nothing.

- S108-4 A COUNCIL THAT AGREES CAN STILL BE MISSING THE CONSTRAINT. Both reviewers independently, over two rounds, said not to build the attic without footage. They were right about the evidence and wrong about the decision, because neither had the WORKING AGREEMENT (the owner asked for it by name) nor the CELLAR PRECEDENT (built entirely from estimates eyeballed against two oil drums, shipped with its caveat, approved). Convergence between two models is not proof; it can be a shared blind spot. The right move was to build it minimal, grade every tile, and record their dissent verbatim rather than laundering it into agreement.

- S108-5 THE GLYPH COLLISION FIRED AGAIN AND THE ASSERT PAID FOR ITSELF. I picked 'd' for the doorstep; 'd' was already the canopy, and a dict literal silently keeps the last definition. The layer assertion caught it in one run. I then stopped guessing and computed the free glyphs from the source. Same class as S107's railing 'T'. The fix is not to be more careful, it is to never hand-pick from a 74-entry namespace.

- S108-6 THE EVIDENCE WAS SITTING IN Downloads THE WHOLE TIME. VID_20260813_092612.mp4 - 165 s, delivered alongside the S106 walk - had never been entered into the database, so five sessions treated that walk as the only narrated footage. Daniel said 'study the videoS'; the plural was the clue. S107 learned the database already held three of four corrections in prose; this is the same lesson about the media shelf. Before believing something does not exist, list the directory.

- S108-7 A GUARD FOR THE FUTURE IS NOT THE SAME SIN AS A GUARD THAT CANNOT FIRE. I rejected the leaf-record rule for firing on 0 claims, then shipped the absent-media rule which also fires on 0 today. The difference is structural: the leaf rule cannot fire because the ontology is flat, while the absent-media rule's invariant simply holds right now - and a planted violation proves it blocks. Keeping that distinction honest is what stops 'a control that cannot fail' becoming an excuse to ship no controls at all.

- S108-8 I FOLLOWED THE LETTER OF THE TRAP AND MISSED ITS TYPE. The handoff warned: populate verification[] in the SAME edit that sets status:completed. I did exactly that - and MCV still hard-failed, because it CRASHED rather than failed: verification[] is read as machine-checkable assertion OBJECTS and I had written eight paragraphs of prose. Doing the ritual is not the same as knowing what the field is for. I then read the verifier's source rather than guessing the schema, and two of my rewritten assertions failed honestly on first run - a grep expecting 3 hits of a tile name that a loop emits once, and a needle spanning a line break. Both were my assertions being wrong about reality, which is the correct direction for a test to fail in.

- S108-9 TWO OF MY OWN FILES DESCRIBED THE SAME STACK AND I NEVER COMPARED THEM. I drew BLD-1's chimneypiece on the north wall because a fireplace facing the camera is easy to read top-down. Daniel marked the real position in red: the partition. He was right and it was CHECKABLE - build-zone1.py puts the chimney on the roof at 4-6 m from the west corner, which is interior col 4 exactly. S107's lesson was that the database held the corrections in prose; this is the same failure between two generators I wrote myself. When two files describe one physical thing, the cross-check is not optional.

- S108-10 CORRECT AND ILLEGIBLE IS STILL WRONG. The rebuilt stack was in the right tile with the right provenance and every guard passed - and it rendered as a thin vertical line, because I had put both openings inside one 16 px tile and given each a 5 px slot. On a north-south wall seen from above a hearth's WIDTH runs up the screen, so it needed to be three tiles tall. Nothing static can catch this: the data was right and the drawing was unreadable. Only looking at it caught it.

- S108-11 THE HONEST COMPROMISE WAS STILL THE WRONG ANSWER. I drew the back door on the roof line because BLD-1 has no north elevation to hang it on, documented that as a drawing convention, and flagged it to Daniel. He X'd it out and asked for it to be INVISIBLE — and his answer is better than mine on every axis: it removes a tile instead of adding one, it stops lying about the building, and it turns a compromise into a secret the player has to learn. Declaring a compromise clearly is not the same as it being the best available design, and 'I wrote down that this is a bit wrong' should not buy it a pass.

- S108-12 'NEVER SEEN' IS A CLAIM WITH AN EXPIRY DATE. The grass in the roof corners came from a ground fill under the house footprint commented 'under the footprint; never seen'. True when written — and false the moment a hipped roof with transparent corners was drawn over it, which I did myself two priorities later. An assumption recorded in a comment does not re-check itself when the thing it depended on changes.

- S108-13 I SILENCED A BUILD AND THEN TRUSTED IT. A slice edit deleted draw_int_door_back, and because I ran the rebuild as `python tools/build-atlas.py >/dev/null 2>&1` the NameError was invisible. Every downstream check then passed against the PREVIOUS atlas still sitting on disk - six guards, tsc, the bundle guard, even the browser, which showed the old art and looked fine. I reported a transparent-ground stove that had never been built. Two rules out of this: never redirect the output of a generator whose product you are about to verify, because a stale artifact passes every check a fresh one would; and prefer anchored edits to index-slice edits, which silently swallow whatever happens to sit between the two anchors. MCV caught it when nothing else did, which is the second time this session it has earned its place.

- S108-14 THE DOMINANT BUG CLASS TODAY WAS 'CORRECT FOR WHERE IT USED TO BE'. The worn path above the cellar mouth, the attic stair head, the tree canopy over the raised store roof, a landmark stranded inside a new lintel, and three MCV assertions — every one was drawn or written correctly for a position something no longer occupied. Moving a feature is never just moving the feature; it invalidates everything positioned RELATIVE to it. The guards caught the ones with machine-checkable relationships (reach, MCV); the eye caught the rest.

- S108-15 A NUMBER FROM THE OWNER IS NOT A NUMBER UNTIL YOU KNOW WHAT IT COUNTS. The store roof took four attempts: 'extend the 1.5 tile height' read as ADD 1.5 tiles (four rows), cut to three, cut to what I counted as two — apex, slope, eave — and was told 'when i said two tiles high i meant two tiles high, you made it three'. He was counting the eave and I was not. The failure was never arithmetic; it was shipping three times without pinning down the unit.

- S108-16 WHEN A FIX KEEPS ALMOST WORKING, RE-READ THE RULE, NOT THE NUMBERS. The chimney's brown outline got three shading attempts — flat fill, pantiles in brick, pantiles shifted down the ramp — each fainter and each still wrong. The constraint was structural: one palette per 8x8 quadrant, and a stack straddling the middle forces BOTH quadrants to BRICK, so the roof beside it can never be ROOF. Moving the stack into one half solved it instantly. Three rounds of tuning is the signal to go back to the constraint.

## 2026-08-14 — Session S107: hygiene settled, provenance made enforceable, and four owner layout corrections (5 priorities, all shipped)

- P1 #pattern-i-picked-the-broader-of-two-options-that-were-not-alternatives — I rejected path-scoping the gitleaks allowlist for a GOOD reason (it would blind the scanner to a real key added to that file later) and then chose a global secret-pattern exemption, which is strictly broader. The two were never alternatives: ANDing them gives both properties at once. GROK-ANALYST caught it at severity 6. When you reject option A for a real weakness, check whether A-and-B together beats B alone before shipping B.

- P1 #pattern-a-null-test-is-not-a-passing-test — My negative control fed a synthetic sk-ant key to a regex anchored on ^claude-. It could never have matched, so its non-detection said nothing about the allowlist — yet I recorded 'allowlist exonerated'. Twice more the same shape: AWS's canonical EXAMPLE keys are stopworded by gitleaks by design, and a regex written for the advisory NUMBER suppressed nothing because the captured secret was the KEY PATH. A control only counts if it would FAIL when the thing under test is broken. Three of my four first-attempt controls could not have failed.

- P1 #pattern-prove-the-semantics-before-relying-on-them — condition="AND" parsed and produced the result I wanted, which is exactly what a silently-ignored key also produces. The decisive test was breaking the path clause on purpose: findings returned under AND, stayed suppressed under OR. Config that accepts your key is not config that honours it. The same probe found targetRules validates rule ids at load, so a typo fails loudly instead of widening the exemption.

- P1 #pattern-read-what-the-tool-captured-instead-of-what-you-assume-it-captured — For the archived-state false positive I assumed generic-api-key had flagged the 7-digit advisory number and wrote ^[0-9]+$. It suppressed nothing. Running the scanner without --redact on that one file showed the secret was `exceptions.2.advisory_id` — the dotted JSON key path. This is S106's 'read the checker, not the cure it suggests' in a new costume, and it cost two wrong regexes before I looked.

- P1 #pattern-the-unredact-must-be-scoped-or-it-scans-everything — Lifting --redact to read that one captured value ran against the whole tree, printing live Anthropic and GCP keys from .env into the transcript. Nothing was committed and .env stays gitignored, but the keys now need rotating (CF-S107-KEY-EXPOSURE). The safe form is --source <the one directory> together with --redact, and only lift --redact on a path already known to hold no real secrets. A debugging convenience became a credential-rotation task.

- P1 #pattern-a-dry-run-earns-its-keep-on-destructive-work — The backlog described the sweep as '21 stale files'. Classifying each one first showed three that must not be touched: a temp carrying THIS session's id, a live non-zombie lockdir, and a temp mid-write by the PID holding it (which failed JSON parse precisely because it was being written). A glob would have deleted the running session's own state machinery. Also: removing lockdir members individually then rmdir means an unexpected member raises instead of being recursively destroyed.

- P1 #pattern-check-that-only-adds-findings-is-decoration — GROK-ANALYST's three findings were all real and all actionable, and the fix was better than the original on every axis. But its severity-3 (bare lockdirs never swept) was WRONG once investigated: lib/lock.sh:58 reclaims a stale bare lock by atomic `mv lockdir zombie.$$`, so sweeping bare locks externally would race that mv, and targeting .zombie.* is correct by design. Accepting a reviewer's findings wholesale is the same failure as ignoring them — each one still has to be checked against the code.

The guard refused all 11 on its first run. The residual is real and recorded: I then chose the replacement ids by judgement, and no static check can catch a wrong-but-plausible citation.

- P2 #pattern-measure-before-choosing-a-threshold-and-be-willing-to-find-none - Grok wanted SSIM>0.97 against re-extracted frames; I countered with 'the measured hex must appear in the palette'.
